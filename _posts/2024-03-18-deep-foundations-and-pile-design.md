Deep foundations transfer structural loads through weak or compressible surface soils to stronger strata at greater depth. They are essential for high-rise buildings, bridges, heavy industrial facilities, and sites with poor near-surface conditions.

## Types of Deep Foundations

### Driven Piles
- Steel H-piles and pipe piles
- Precast concrete piles
- Timber piles
- Composite piles

Driven piles are installed by impact or vibratory hammers. They densify surrounding granular soils and provide good quality control through driving records.

### Drilled Shafts (Bored Piles)
- Constructed by excavating a hole and filling it with reinforced concrete
- Can achieve large diameters and high capacities
- Suitable for a wide range of soil and rock conditions
- Require careful attention to construction quality (slurry, casing, concrete placement)

### Other Systems
- Continuous Flight Auger (CFA) piles
- Micropiles
- Helical piles
- Caissons

## Axial Capacity

The ultimate axial capacity of a pile is the sum of shaft resistance and base resistance:

$$Q_u = Q_s + Q_b$$

where:
- $Q_s$ = skin friction along the shaft
- $Q_b$ = end-bearing at the tip

### Shaft Resistance
For cohesive soils (α-method):

$$Q_s = \alpha \, s_u \, A_s$$

For cohesionless soils (β-method):

$$Q_s = \beta \, \sigma'_v \, A_s$$

### Base Resistance
Depends on the strength of the material at the tip and the method of installation.

## Settlement and Group Effects

Single pile settlement is usually small if the pile is founded in competent material. However, pile groups can experience additional settlement due to interaction and the compression of the soil mass within the group.

Group efficiency factors and equivalent raft methods are commonly used to estimate group capacity and settlement.

## Lateral Loading

Piles must often resist lateral loads from wind, earthquakes, or earth pressures. Analysis methods include:

- Broms’ method (simplified)
- p-y curve approaches (non-linear soil springs)
- Finite-element modeling

## Load Testing

Static load tests remain the most reliable method of verifying capacity. Dynamic testing (e.g., PDA) and rapid load tests offer faster alternatives. Integrity testing (PIT, CSL, thermal integrity profiling) is used to assess the quality of installed piles.

## Construction Considerations

- Selection of appropriate installation method for the ground conditions
- Management of groundwater and slurry
- Quality control of concrete and reinforcement
- Monitoring of driving stresses or drilling parameters
- Handling of unexpected ground conditions (boulders, artesian pressures, etc.)

## Design Process Summary

1. Define structural loads and performance criteria
2. Characterize the subsurface profile and select design parameters
3. Select pile type and preliminary dimensions
4. Calculate axial and lateral capacities
5. Check settlement and group effects
6. Finalize structural design of the pile and pile cap
7. Specify testing and quality control requirements

## Final Thoughts

Deep foundation design is both a science and an art. It requires sound theoretical knowledge, careful interpretation of site data, and practical judgment informed by construction experience. When executed well, deep foundations provide reliable, long-term support for the most demanding structures.
