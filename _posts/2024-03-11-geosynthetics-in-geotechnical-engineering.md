Geosynthetics are synthetic polymeric materials used in geotechnical, environmental, and civil engineering applications. Since their introduction in the 1960s and 1970s, they have become indispensable tools for improving performance and economy in a wide range of projects.

## Types of Geosynthetics

| Type | Primary Functions | Common Materials |
|------|-------------------|------------------|
| **Geotextiles** | Separation, filtration, reinforcement, protection | Polypropylene, polyester |
| **Geogrids** | Reinforcement | Polyester, polypropylene, HDPE |
| **Geomembranes** | Containment (barriers) | HDPE, LLDPE, PVC |
| **Geonets** | Drainage | HDPE |
| **Geocomposites** | Combined functions | Various combinations |
| **Geocells** | Confinement and reinforcement | HDPE, polypropylene |
| **Geopipes** | Drainage | HDPE, PVC |

## Primary Functions

### Separation
Prevent intermixing of dissimilar soils (e.g., aggregate base and soft subgrade).

### Filtration
Allow water flow while retaining soil particles. Critical in drainage applications and behind retaining walls.

### Drainage
Convey water within the plane of the geosynthetic (geonets and geocomposites).

### Reinforcement
Provide tensile strength to soil, enabling steeper slopes, higher embankments, and more economical retaining walls.

### Containment
Create low-permeability barriers for landfills, ponds, and contaminated sites (geomembranes).

### Protection
Cushion geomembranes against puncture from sharp aggregates or uneven surfaces.

## Key Applications

- **Reinforced soil walls and slopes** — MSE walls using geogrids or geotextiles
- **Embankments on soft ground** — basal reinforcement to improve stability and reduce settlement
- **Road and railway construction** — separation and reinforcement of subgrades
- **Landfill engineering** — liner and cover systems
- **Erosion control** — temporary and permanent protection of slopes and channels
- **Drainage systems** — edge drains, wall drains, and gas venting layers

## Design Considerations

Successful use of geosynthetics requires attention to:

- **Mechanical properties** — tensile strength, stiffness, creep behavior
- **Hydraulic properties** — permittivity, transmissivity, apparent opening size
- **Durability** — resistance to UV, chemical degradation, and installation damage
- **Interface properties** — friction and adhesion with adjacent soils
- **Installation practices** — proper placement, seaming, and protection from damage

Design methods range from simple empirical approaches to sophisticated limit-equilibrium and finite-element analyses that explicitly model the geosynthetic as a tensile element.

## Advantages

- Cost-effective compared with traditional materials
- Rapid installation
- Consistent quality (factory-produced)
- Ability to enable construction on difficult sites
- Reduced environmental impact in many applications

## Conclusion

Geosynthetics have transformed many areas of geotechnical practice. When properly selected and designed, they provide reliable, economical solutions for reinforcement, drainage, filtration, and containment challenges.
