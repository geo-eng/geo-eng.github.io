Retaining walls are structures designed to support soil at different elevations on either side. They are essential for creating level platforms, supporting excavations, and stabilizing slopes in constrained sites.

## Types of Retaining Walls

Common types include:

- **Gravity walls** — rely on self-weight for stability
- **Cantilever walls** — reinforced concrete walls with a base slab
- **Counterfort walls** — used for greater heights
- **Sheet pile walls** — flexible walls driven into the ground
- **Soldier pile and lagging** — common for temporary excavations
- **Mechanically Stabilized Earth (MSE) walls** — soil reinforced with geosynthetics or metallic strips
- **Soil nail walls** — in-situ reinforcement of existing ground

## Lateral Earth Pressure Theories

The design of retaining walls depends on accurate estimation of lateral earth pressures.

### Rankine Theory
Assumes a smooth vertical wall and a horizontal backfill. Active and passive earth pressure coefficients are:

$$K_a = \tan^2\left(45^\circ - \frac{\phi'}{2}\right)$$

$$K_p = \tan^2\left(45^\circ + \frac{\phi'}{2}\right)$$

### Coulomb Theory
Accounts for wall friction and sloping backfill. It is more general than Rankine’s theory but still assumes a planar failure surface.

### At-Rest Pressure
When the wall does not move, the at-rest coefficient $K_0$ is used:

$$K_0 = 1 - \sin\phi' \quad \text{(for normally consolidated soils)}$$

## Design Considerations

A complete retaining wall design must check:

1. **External stability**
   - Sliding
   - Overturning
   - Bearing capacity
   - Global stability

2. **Internal stability** (for reinforced soil walls)
   - Tensile strength of reinforcement
   - Pullout resistance
   - Connection strength

3. **Drainage**
   - Water pressure behind the wall can dramatically increase loads
   - Proper drainage (weep holes, gravel backfill, geocomposites) is essential

## Seismic Earth Pressures

Under earthquake loading, additional dynamic earth pressures develop. The Mononobe-Okabe method is a widely used pseudostatic approach for estimating seismic active and passive pressures.

## Modern Trends

MSE walls and soil nail walls have become highly popular due to their economy, flexibility, and relatively rapid construction. They also perform well under seismic loading when properly designed.

## Conclusion

Successful retaining wall design requires a clear understanding of earth pressure theory, careful attention to drainage and backfill placement, and thorough stability checks.
